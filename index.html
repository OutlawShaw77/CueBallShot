Pool shot: which directions send the cue ball into the red ball?

The cue ball starts at (0, 0) and the red ball sits at (0, 5), both with radius 1. Rails run along x = 5, x = −5, and y = 10, and the bottom is open. Strike the cue ball at any angle. The shot counts the moment the cue ball touches the red ball, however many rails it bounces off first.

The math

Track the cue ball’s center. It touches the red ball when the two centers are 1 + 1 = 2 apart, so the red ball acts like a target of radius 2. A ray from the origin at angle θ reaches a target of radius r centered at (a, b) when |a sin θ − b cos θ| ≤ r.

The cue ball’s edge meets a rail when its center is 1 unit away, so the center bounces off the shrunken rails x = ±4 and y = 9 (the dashed box on the table). To handle bounces, reflect the red ball across those rails and fire a straight ray at the images.

The balls are treated as ideal: no friction, no spin, perfect rail bounces. Shots aimed downward leave through the open bottom, and the simulator stops after 400 bounces, so angles under about 0.4° can show as misses.
